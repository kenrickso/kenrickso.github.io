Shivering, nauseous, and seeing my breath for the first time, I felt my body freeze the moment I stepped outside in Belgium. For someone from a tropical country, the extreme cold was a shock. It was not just uncomfortable, it felt physically overwhelming. In that moment, weather stopped being just numbers on a screen and became a lived experience. It was a time when I experienced how climate can affect our health.

As a baseline, we start with a purely statistical mortality model that captures patterns across age, time, and season, but does not incorporate any external drivers. The weekly seasonal model is

\begin{equation}
\ln(\mu*{x,t,w,r}) = \alpha*{x,r} + \beta*x \kappa*{t,r} + \gamma*x \lambda*{w,r},
\label{eq:wlc_model}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_{x,r}$ represents baseline mortality for age $x$ in region $r$, $\kappa_{t,r}$ captures long-term trends, and $\lambda_{w,r}$ represents seasonal variation. Age-specific coefficients $\beta_x$ and $\gamma_x$ modulate responsiveness to these effects. While this model captures statistical patterns in mortality, it is limited because it does not account for exogenous factors such as climate, which can drive short-term deviations and delayed effects in mortality.
